%N Number of steps after which the Collatz trajectories of A078417(n) and A078417(n)+1 first coincide.
%C With f(x) = x/2 for even x and 3*x+1 for odd x (A006370), a(n) is the least k such that f^k(m) = f^k(m+1), where m = A078417(n). Such a k exists and a(n) <= A006577(m), because m and m+1 reach 1 after the same number of steps.
%C a(n) >= 3, with equality exactly when A078417(n) == 4 (mod 8). These are Garner's pairs (8*k+4, 8*k+5), which meet at 6*k+4 after three steps.
%C a(n) is never 4. On each residue class m == r (mod 16), f^4(m) and f^4(m+1) are affine functions of m; they coincide for some m only when r = 4 or r = 12, and then for every m in the class, but those pairs already meet after three steps.
%C In the form recorded by Lagarias, Gao's conjecture (see A400486) states that the numbers m with f^k(m) = f^k(m+1) for some k <= log_2(m) have natural density 1. Among the m that reach 1, these are exactly the terms m = A078417(n) with a(n) <= log_2(m).
%H Guo-Gang Gao, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(93)90240-T">On consecutive numbers of the same height in the Collatz problem</a>, Discrete Math. 112 (1993), 261-267.
%H Lynn E. Garner, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(85)80020-0">On heights in the Collatz 3n+1 problem</a>, Discrete Math. 55 (1985), 57-64.
%H Jeffrey C. Lagarias, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309224">The 3x+1 problem: An annotated bibliography (1963-1999) (sorted by author)</a>, arXiv:math/0309224 [math.NT], 2003-2011 (entries 68 and 72).
%e a(1) = 3: A078417(1) = 12, and 12 -> 6 -> 3 -> 10, 13 -> 40 -> 20 -> 10.
%e a(2) = 9: A078417(2) = 14, and the trajectories 14, 7, 22, 11, 34, 17, 52, 26, 13, 40 and 15, 46, 23, 70, 35, 106, 53, 160, 80, 40 first agree at step 9.
