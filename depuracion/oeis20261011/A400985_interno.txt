%N Length of the longest run of consecutive integers in [1, 2^n) that take the same number of Collatz steps to reach 1.
%C The steps are those of the Collatz map x -> x/2 for even x, 3*x+1 for odd x (A006370), and their number is A006577.
%C Wu (1992) studied the runs of consecutive integers with the same number of steps in [1, 2^N), counting the integers that lie in runs of length at least 2 (A400487). He found that the longest run below 2^30 has 176 integers, starting at 722067240, and conjectured that the length of the longest run in [1, M) tends to infinity with M.
%C Since a(n) is the largest k with A078441(k) + k <= 2^n, Wu's conjecture is the statement that A078441(k) exists for every k, that is, Garner's conjecture that there are arbitrarily long runs of consecutive integers with the same height.
%C a(27)-a(31) were obtained from the b-file of A078441.
%H Jeffrey C. Lagarias, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309224">The 3x+1 problem: An annotated bibliography (1963-1999) (sorted by author)</a>, arXiv:math/0309224 [math.NT], 2003-2011 (entries 72 and 188).
%H Jia-Bang Wu, <a href="https://doi.org/10.13245/j.hust.1992.05.030">A tendency of the proportion of consecutive numbers of the same height in the Collatz problem</a> (in Chinese), J. Huazhong Univ. Sci. Tech. 20 (1992), no. 5, 171-174.
%F a(n) = max{k : A078441(k) + k <= 2^n}.
%e a(4) = 2: below 16 the runs of integers with the same number of steps have length at most 2, for instance 12 and 13 (9 steps each) and 14 and 15 (17 steps each).
%e a(5) = 3: 28, 29 and 30 take 18 steps each.
