A078441
a(n) begins the first chain of n consecutive positive integers that have equal h-values, where h(k) is the length of the finite sequence k, f(k), f(f(k)), ...., 1 in the Collatz (or 3x + 1) problem. (The earliest "1" is meant.)
(history; edit; published version)

#31 by Omar Said at Sun Sep 27 16:54:17 EDT 2026
COMMENTS
Garner (1985) conjectured that there are arbitrarily long runs of consecutive integers with the same height. As recalled by Gao (1993), the runs should have the same height, computed with f as here, and the same number of halving steps (A006666); in that form the conjecture implies that a(n) exists for every n. - Omar Said, Sep 27 2026
a(7), a(17), a(25), a(40), a(65), a(98) and a(176) are the starting points of the first seven runs in Table 1 of Gao (1993), which lists the longest run in each range 10^j..10^(j+1); in each of these runs all members also have the same number of halving steps (A006666). The run of 176 integers from a(176) = 722067240 is the longest below 2^30, as found by Wu (1992). - Omar Said, Sep 27 2026

(PARI) collatz(x) = {my(t=x, m=0, k=0); while(t>1, if(t%2, t*=3; t++; m++); t>>=1; k++); m+k; }
list(n) = {my(v= vector(n), t0=1, k=0); while(k<n, my(t=t0+1, c=collatz(t0), j); while(collatz(t)==c, t++); j=t-t0; while(k<j && k<n, if(v[k+1]==0, v[k+1]= t0; k++)); t0=t); v; } \\ V. Barbera, Sep 28 2026

a(n) begins the first chain of n consecutive positive integers that have equal h-values, where h(k) is the length of the finite sequence k, f(k), f(f(k)), ...., 1 in the Collatz (or 3x + 1) problem. (The earliest "1" is meant.)

EXAMPLE
28, 29, 30 is the first chain of three consecutive positive integers n, n+1, n+2 such that h(n) = h(n+1) = h(n+2). Hence a(3)=28.
