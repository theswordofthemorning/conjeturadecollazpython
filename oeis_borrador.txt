--- LOS CAMPOS, EN ORDEN, CON SU SIGNIFICADO ---

Cada linea tiene la forma:   %x Annnnnn <contenido>
donde x es la letra del campo y Annnnnn el numero de catalogo. Al enviar una
sucesion nueva el A-number se asigna solo (y se puede reservar un bloque de
A-numbers de antemano, que es lo que conviene aqui porque las dos entradas se
referencian mutuamente).

  %I  Identificacion. OBLIGATORIO. Solo el A-number (los Mnnnn/Nnnnn son de
      los libros de 1973 y 1995; no aplican a una sucesion nueva).
  %S  Primera linea de datos. OBLIGATORIO. Minimo 4 terminos. Enteros
      separados por comas, SIN espacios ni tabuladores.
  %T  Segunda linea de datos (opcional).
  %U  Tercera linea de datos (opcional). No hay %V/%W: son solo tres.
  %N  Nombre / definicion. OBLIGATORIO. UNA sola linea, solo una permitida.
      Puede ser larga. a(n) denota el n-esimo termino.
  %C  Comentarios. Varias lineas permitidas.
  %D  Referencias bibliograficas detalladas (libros, articulos SIN enlace).
      Una por linea. Alfabetizadas por apellido del primer autor.
  %H  Enlaces. Una por linea, con la forma exacta
          %H Annnnnn Autor, <a href="URL">Titulo</a>, resto de la cita.
      Alfabetizados por apellido; el b-file va siempre el primero.
  %F  Formulas, recurrencias, funciones generatrices.
  %e  Ejemplos que ilustran los primeros terminos (minuscula, no %E).
      La hoja de estilo pide 1 a 3 casos simples y "show your work"; y avisa
      de no poner un ejemplo que solo repita la definicion.
  %p  Programa Maple.   %t  Programa Mathematica.   %o  Otro lenguaje
      (con el lenguaje entre parentesis al principio: "(Python) ...").
  %Y  Referencias cruzadas a otras sucesiones ("Cf. A000001, A000002.").
  %K  Keywords. OBLIGATORIO.
  %O  Offset. OBLIGATORIO. DOS numeros separados por coma.
  %A  Autor / quien lo envia. OBLIGATORIO.
  %E  Extensiones, correcciones, ediciones posteriores.

--- LIMITE DE LONGITUD DE %S / %T / %U ---

De la hoja de estilo (seccion Data) y de eishelp1.html:

  - Como maximo TRES lineas de datos: %S, %T y %U. "Don't give more than 3
    lines."
  - Minimo 4 terminos.
  - El objetivo es llenar unas tres lineas de pantalla, "maybe 260 characters
    including digits, signs, commas and whitespaces". Y textualmente:
    "260 characters is just a guide. [...] Try to stay under 500 chars".
  - Si ya hay tres lineas llenas, no se anaden mas terminos a %S/%T/%U: se
    crea un B-FILE (fichero b<numero>.txt con "n a(n)" una pareja por linea),
    que se sube aparte y se enlaza desde %H.
  - En la practica cada linea fisica se corta a ~70 caracteres.

  ESTE CASO: la sucesion a son 148 caracteres y la b son 104. Las dos caben
  MUY por debajo de los 260, y por eso las dos llevan la keyword "more".

--- QUE VALORES DE %K CORRESPONDEN AQUI ---

  nonn   SI. Todos los terminos son >= 0. Es el minimo obligatorio
         (hay que declarar "nonn" o "sign").
  more   SI. "More terms are needed! Would someone please extend this
         sequence?" Con 26 y 25 terminos no se llenan las tres lineas.
  hard   NO, y conviene NO ponerla. "hard" es "next term is not known and may
         be hard to find". Aqui a(29) se calcula con 2^29 restos en unos
         minutos de CPU; es caro, no dificil. Ponerlo seria exagerar.
  base   NO. "base" es para sucesiones que dependen de la BASE de escritura
         (propiedades de digitos). Aqui el 2^k no es una eleccion de notacion:
         es la estructura del propio mapa de Collatz (el vector de paridad de
         Terras). Un editor podria discutirlo; el argumento esta aqui.
  easy   NO. "It is easy to produce terms of this sequence". El algoritmo es
         simple pero el coste es 2^n: a(35) ya no es "easy".
  nice, core, look, hear   NO. Las pone el editor, no el que envia.
  new / changed   NO. Las pone el sistema automaticamente.

  DECISION:  %K nonn,more     en las dos entradas.

--- COMO SE INDICA EL OFFSET %O ---

Dos numeros, "a,b":

  a = subindice del PRIMER termino mostrado.
  b = posicion (contando desde 1) del primer termino cuyo valor absoluto es
      >= 2; si no hay ninguno, b = 1. El segundo numero lo usa el sistema
      para ordenar lexicograficamente; en la practica lo ajusta solo, pero
      hay que ponerlo bien.

  Sucesion a: empieza en k = 3, y los terminos son 1, 3, 8, ...  El primero
              >= 2 es el 3, en posicion 2.        ->   %O 3,2
  Sucesion b: empieza en k = 3, y los terminos son 1, 2, 2, ... El primero
              >= 2 es el 2, en posicion 2.        ->   %O 3,2

  ALTERNATIVA QUE UN EDITOR PUEDE PEDIR. Comprobado con una implementacion
  en Python puro: a(0) = a(1) = a(2) = 0. O sea que la sucesion esta definida
  desde k = 0 y los tres primeros terminos son cero. Si el editor prefiere no
  tener un offset "artificial", las lineas serian:

      %S <A-a> 0,0,1,3,8,18,39,82,170,351,721,1476,3012,6130,12450,25241
      %O <A-a> 1,4
      (con k = 1..; el primer termino >= 2 es el 3, en posicion 4)

      %S <A-b> 0,1,1,2,2,3,4,6,11,19,34,60,106,190,341,623,1148,2124
      %O <A-b> 1,4

--- SOBRE EL PUNTO FINAL DEL %N ---

%N <Aa> Number of residues r mod 2^n such that the trajectories of 2^n*m + r and 2^n*m + r + 1 under the accelerated Collatz map T (T(x) = (3*x+1)/2 if x is odd, T(x) = x/2 if x is even) agree from step n onwards for every m >= 1.
%C <Aa> Equivalently, a(n) counts the residues r mod 2^n for which S_n(r) = S_n(r+1) and T^n(r) = T^n(r+1), where S_n(x) is the number of odd steps among the first n steps of T. This is a finite check that decides the behavior of infinitely many m, because of Terras's parity vector theorem: T^n(x) = (3^S_n(x)/2^n)*x + rho_n(x), where both S_n and rho_n depend only on x mod 2^n, so that T^n(2^n*m + r) = 3^S_n(r)*m + T^n(r) is a line in m whose slope and intercept depend only on r, and two lines agree for all m exactly when both coefficients agree.
%C <Aa> The wrap-around case r = 2^n - 1 is included: its successor 2^n*m + 2^n lies in class 0 of the next block, so the condition there reads S_n(2^n-1) = S_n(0) and T^n(2^n-1) = 3^S_n(0) + T^n(0).
%C <Aa> If two trajectories agree at step n they are the same from then on, so the two integers have the same Collatz height (A006577); the condition on S_n also forces them to have spent the same number of steps of the classical map getting there, since n steps of T are n + S_n steps of the classical 3x+1 map.
%C <Aa> THE CRITERION IS NOT NEW. It is Terras's parity vector theorem (1976) applied to consecutive integers, and the families it selects are published: r = 4 mod 8, i.e. the pairs (8*m+4, 8*m+5), is the base case of Garner (1985) and is proved again in Elia and Tucker (2015); r = 5 mod 32, i.e. the pairs (32*m+5, 32*m+6), is attributed to Wu and Huang (2001). This sequence is only the COUNT of the classes that the published criterion selects; it is not a new result. It appears not to have been tabulated before.
%C <Aa> a(n)/2^n is a proved lower bound for the density of pairs (x, x+1) of equal Collatz height, decided by the residue alone: a(32)/2^32 = 0.435658... The measured density of such pairs up to 2^28 is about 0.5186, so about 8 percentage points come from pairs that merge for their particular m without their whole residue class merging.
%C <Aa> a(n+1) >= 2*a(n) for all n, hence a(n)/2^n is nondecreasing. Proof in three lines: with m = 1 the formula above gives T^n(2^n + r) = 3^S_n(r) + T^n(r), and the same for r+1; if r merges mod 2^n then S_n(r) = S_n(r+1) and T^n(r) = T^n(r+1), so 2^n + r and 2^n + r + 1 also have equal S_n and equal T^n; therefore both r and r + 2^n merge mod 2^(n+1). Monotonicity is thus forced by construction, not an empirical finding.
%C <Aa> The excess over the doubling is <Ab>(n) = a(n+1) - 2*a(n), the classes that first merge at level n+1. Since a(n+1)/2^(n+1) - a(n)/2^n = <Ab>(n)/2^(n+1), the limit of a(n)/2^n equals a(n)/2^n + Sum_{j>=n} <Ab>(j)/2^(j+1), so the whole question of where the density bound goes reduces to the growth rate of <Ab>. The measured ratio <Ab>(n)/<Ab>(n-1) rises monotonically (1.8427 at n = 17, 1.9269 at n = 27, 1.9390 at n = 31) with no sign of settling; if it stays below 2 the sum converges and the bound has a limit < 1, if it reaches 2 the bound tends to 1.
%C <Aa> a(0) = a(1) = a(2) = 0, which is why the offset is 3.
%D <Aa> L. E. Garner, On heights in the Collatz 3n+1 problem, Discrete Mathematics 55 (1985), 57-64.

%N <Ab> Number of residues mod 2^(n+1) that first merge at level n+1 in the Collatz problem: a(n) = <Aa>(n+1) - 2*<Aa>(n), where <Aa>(n) is the number of residues r mod 2^n such that the trajectories of 2^n*m + r and 2^n*m + r + 1 under the accelerated Collatz map agree from step n onwards for every m >= 1.
%C <Ab> Every residue that merges mod 2^n gives two residues that merge mod 2^(n+1), namely r and r + 2^n, so <Aa>(n+1) >= 2*<Aa>(n) is forced. Proof in three lines: from T^n(2^n*m + r) = 3^S_n(r)*m + T^n(r), taking m = 1 gives T^n(2^n + r) = 3^S_n(r) + T^n(r), and the same for r+1; if r merges then S_n(r) = S_n(r+1) and T^n(r) = T^n(r+1), so 2^n + r and 2^n + r + 1 agree too. This sequence counts the surplus, i.e. the classes that are NOT inherited from the level below.
%C <Ab> This is the sequence with the content, because the monotonicity of <Aa>(n)/2^n is trivial while its limit is not. Writing f(n) = <Aa>(n)/2^n, one has f(n+1) - f(n) = a(n)/2^(n+1) and hence f(infinity) = f(n) + Sum_{j>=n} a(j)/2^(j+1). Since f(n) is a proved lower bound for the density of pairs of consecutive integers with the same Collatz height, the whole question of where that bound goes is the growth rate of this sequence: if a(n+1)/a(n) stays below 2 the sum converges and the bound has a limit < 1; if it reaches 2 the sum diverges and the bound tends to 1.
%C <Ab> The measured ratio a(n)/a(n-1) rises monotonically and does not settle: 1.8427 at n = 17, 1.8802 at n = 20, 1.8886 at n = 22, 1.8973 at n = 23, 1.9127 at n = 24, 1.9180 at n = 25, 1.9230 at n = 26, 1.9269 at n = 27, 1.9306 at n = 28, 1.9337 at n = 29, 1.9364 at n = 30, 1.9390 at n = 31. The increments of that ratio do shrink (0.0050, 0.0039, 0.0037, 0.0031, 0.0027, 0.0026) but too slowly to tell geometric decay, which would give a limit near 1.95, from 1/n decay, which would reach 2. With these terms the question cannot be decided either way, which is the point of tabulating the sequence.
