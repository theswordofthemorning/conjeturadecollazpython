This paper does not concern the 3x+1 conjecture, that is, whether every trajectory reaches 1; it concerns how often the trajectories of two consecutive integers meet. Let C be the Collatz map, C(n)=n/2 for even n and C(n)=3n+1 for odd n. Following Gao (1993), n and n+1 coalesce if C^k(n)=C^k(n+1) for some k, with the same number of halvings on both trajectories and without either trajectory passing through 1. Gao proved that the normalized count of the n<2^k-1 that coalesce within k steps is nondecreasing, and conjectured that it tends to 1. In the form recorded by Lagarias, the conjecture states that the set of n with C^k(n)=C^k(n+1) for some k<=log_2(n) has natural density one. We prove both forms, and that the proportion d(x) of n<x for which n and n+1 coalesce tends to 1. The proof follows a pair of trajectories through a Markov chain driven by fair coin flips, which describes the residue classes modulo 2^t exactly, and shows that the chain is absorbed almost surely, using a square-root drift, a harmonic function, a universal absorbing word and a counting argument for repeated trials that never conditions on the future. Every step is formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, and the final theorems depend only on the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The development was written with extensive assistance from large language models; we describe how it was checked. The results concern coalescence only: they do not show that any integer reaches 1.
